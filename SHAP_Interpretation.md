# SHAP interpretation — SuperKart Random Forest

## How to read the explanation

The fitted Random Forest predicts `Product_Store_Sales_Total` for one product-store record. SHAP values are expressed in the same units as that target. A positive contribution pushes the prediction above the model’s reference output; a negative contribution pulls it below. Global importance is summarized using mean absolute SHAP, so it measures typical contribution magnitude rather than direction. The values below were computed with TreeSHAP on all 1,753 holdout records; the local-explanation explorer uses a reproducible 800-record sample.

## Main drivers

1. **Product MRP is the strongest individual driver.** Its mean absolute SHAP contribution is approximately **409.7 revenue units** per row. In the lowest MRP quintile (average MRP about 105), mean SHAP is about **−468**; in the highest quintile (average MRP about 191), it is about **+511**. In this dataset, higher prices generally move the predicted revenue upward, with a threshold-like/nonlinear pattern learned by the trees. This is an association in the data, not proof that changing price alone will produce the same lift.
2. **Product weight is the next strongest individual numeric driver.** Mean absolute SHAP is approximately **152.1**. The lowest weight quintile has a mean contribution around **−126**, while the highest is around **+252**. Higher-weight items generally receive a higher predicted revenue contribution here. Weight may also proxy for product/pack characteristics not explicitly represented in the file.
3. **Store profile is highly influential as a *joint context*.** Store ID, store type, city tier, size, and establishment year are fixed or nearly fixed together for the four outlets. When their SHAP contributions are summed per row, the mean absolute contribution is approximately **499.5** in the 800-row explanation sample—comparable to the combined contribution of MRP and weight (about **485.3**). The summed store-context contribution averages roughly **+1,203** for OUT003 and **−1,533** for OUT002 in that sample; OUT001 is about **+206** and OUT004 about **−96**. The model is learning large historical outlet-level differences.
4. **Do not treat the separate store variables as independent effects.** For example, OUT002 is the Food Mart, Small, Tier 3 location, and OUT003 is the Departmental Store, Medium, Tier 1 location. Because these attributes are effectively aliases for store identity, TreeSHAP may distribute credit among correlated/redundant fields. The result does **not** show that city tier, size, or store format independently causes the revenue difference. There are only four stores, so generalization to new outlets is especially uncertain.
5. **Product category and sugar labels have comparatively small incremental contribution** after the model sees MRP, weight, and store profile. On the original-field aggregation, mean absolute SHAP is about **8.0** for `Product_Type`, **1.8** for `Product_Sugar_Content`, and **1.2** for the product-ID prefix. `Product_Allocated_Area` is also small (about **5.9**). That does not contradict Fruits and Vegetables being the top category by total historical revenue: category totals depend on how many records/items are represented, while SHAP describes a row-level prediction conditional on the other fields.

## Why the results should be used carefully

The feature effects are predictive associations learned from a small, cross-sectional dataset—not causal estimates or an intervention policy. `Product_MRP` and `Product_Weight` are correlated, and store descriptors are redundant; attribution among correlated inputs can move when the feature set or data distribution changes. The dataset has no dates, units sold, margin, promotions, inventory, stockouts, or repeated time periods. Consequently, the model and SHAP plots support product-store revenue estimation for the represented outlets, not an independently validated quarter-ahead forecast. For operational decisions, collect dated sales and inventory signals, include more stores and periods, validate chronologically, and monitor explanations and errors by store and product group.

The interactive dashboard includes an overall SHAP ranking, a direction/beeswarm view, and a per-record waterfall. In the waterfall, contributions plus the model’s baseline reconstruct the selected prediction (subject to rounding).
